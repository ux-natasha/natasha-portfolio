## Overall Impression

The best-built portfolio site I have reviewed in this system, wrapped around a case study whose most persuasive material is either unwritten or three interactions below where a reviewer will ever look.

You asked whether it satisfies "get attention and get hired." Split those, because the answer differs:

- **Get attention: yes, decisively.** Thirty seconds on this site tells a design lead that you have taste, restraint, and unusual engineering discipline. The dark-mode derivation alone is a hiring signal.
- **Get hired: not yet.** A hiring manager's actual question is *"what did she change, and can she do it here?"* The site currently answers the first half with `one line — not written yet`.

The single biggest opportunity: **the Outcome field.** It is empty in the opening at-a-glance card, empty in the VPro masthead, and empty in the Motion Dime masthead. Three of the site's most load-bearing slots — the ones a reviewer's eye goes to first, because you correctly put them above the fold — are literally labelled as blank. Everything else on this list is secondary to filling those.

## What's Working

**1. The depth model, once you open it.** I expanded every `more ↓` on `/vpro`. Level 2 is excellent — the Configuration passage ("three bespoke patterns is exactly the kind of fragmentation that made the old system take two days to touch a config… the two days became a screen you open yourself") does in one paragraph what most portfolios fail to do in a full case study. It is concrete, it has a stake, it credits the sequencing decision rather than the pixels. This is the asset. It is closed by default and cued by a 9px gray dot.

**2. The credit register.** `SOLO · ENTIRELY MADE BY ME` / `SURVIVOR · TEAM ITERATED, ONE LIVED` / `SHARED · THE HONEST ONE`, set in mono at the right edge of each chapter head. This is the most differentiated thing on the site after the craft — it is a claim a competitor can't copy without also having been honest. It reads instantly, it scans at a glance, and "the honest one" is exactly the register the brief asks for.

**3. Responsive and dark-mode behavior is not an afterthought.** At 390 the rail becomes a horizontal top strip with the same typographic logic — not a hamburger, not a hidden nav. Dark mode holds the accent contract (3:1 marks, 4.5:1 type) rather than relaxing it. Both were checked by eye; both hold.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The Outcome slot is empty in all three places it appears

**Why it matters.** You built the information architecture a hiring manager needs — Role / Timeline / Team / Owned / **Outcome**, above the fold, never behind a disclosure. That is the right structure and most portfolios don't have it. Then the payoff field says `one line — not written yet` in italic stub voice, on the opening screen, on `/vpro`, and on `/motion-dime`. A reviewer who reads only the panels — and many will — leaves knowing your role and nothing about your effect. Worse, the placeholder is *conspicuous*: the corner tag says `placeholder` in rose, which is one of the only two rose elements in the first viewport. The eye goes straight to the one thing that isn't there.

**Fix.** This is a copy task, not a design task, and the facts already exist in your own source. `01-configuration.md` level 2 says the two days became a screen you open yourself. `02-planning.md` says seven or eight prototypes and one survived to production. Those *are* outcomes. Write the opening card's Outcome as one line in the same register as the rest — a number, a stake, or a specific noun — and write VPro's. For Motion Dime, either write the five masthead fields or don't publish the masthead panel on that route yet.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The index opens the folder on chapter 00 — the one chapter you explicitly did not own

**Why it matters.** The cover is a teaser (§1e) and its default state is `fi = 0`, which loads `00 · The old system`, tagged `TEAM · PROLOGUE`. So the first substantive thing a reviewer previews is context work, credited to the team, about a system that no longer exists. Meanwhile `01 Configuration` — tagged `SOLO · ENTIRELY MADE BY ME`, carrying the strongest fact in the whole project — sits behind a tab click. You have correctly refused to reorder chapters away from build order; that rule governs the *record route*, where sequence carries meaning. It does not have to govern which folder the *teaser* opens on. A trailer doesn't open on scene one because the film does.

**Fix.** Default the index cover's selected folder to the compartment's strongest chapter, declared in `compartment.md` as something like `lead: "01"` (defaulting to `0` when absent, so nothing else changes). The tab row still shows build order, still starts at 00, and a reader thumbing left lands exactly where they do today. `/vpro` is untouched.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

### [P1] Four parallel navigations to the same three destinations

**Why it matters.** On the index alone: the rail numerals (01/02/03), the record tab strip (RECORD 01/02/03), and the drawer shelves (VPRO / STACKS / DIME, each with `OPEN →`) all target the same three projects, and the drawer sits ~700px below the strip showing the identical list under a different noun. Add the Catalogue panel on record routes and the next/previous pager and it is five. This was coherent when the drawer held one real folder and two dimmed slots — §1c's "brief moment you pass *through*." At N=3 the drawer has become a second index, which is exactly the failure mode CLAUDE.md's own RISK note predicted. The cost is not confusion (nothing is broken) — it is that `read more →`, the one link that actually matters, competes with four other ways to leave, and the page's rhythm flattens because the same list appears twice.

Related: the shelf pages (`/writing`, `/reading`) each list all three records again under "Written, and worth reading" — a sixth instance.

**Fix.** Pick one. My recommendation: keep the tab strip + cover as the index's only project navigation, and reduce the drawer to what §1c actually asks for — the *future* shelves plus the address. The records are already above it; the drawer's job is to show that more is coming, not to re-list what you just saw. That restores the drawer to a passage rather than a destination and gives `read more →` an uncontested field.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P1] The site's best writing is hidden behind the quietest control on the page

**Why it matters.** Collapsed, each chapter on `/vpro` is: numeral, title, three-line lede, four meta fields, `· more ↓` in 9px gray mono at the bottom-left of an otherwise empty band. The whole flagship case study reads in about ninety seconds and totals ~3,200px. Expanded, it roughly doubles and becomes genuinely good. §1's "closed by default" rule is right and I am not asking you to break it — but the rule says the affordance should be *quiet*, and this one is closer to *invisible*: it is the smallest type on the page, in the lowest-contrast gray, at the far left of a column whose right 45% is empty. The 10-second reader is served. The 10-minute reader is served. The 2-minute reader — the actual hiring manager, who would open one or two — has no reason to think there is anything under it.

A second, compounding problem: the labels differ per chapter ("more", "what i touched", "what lives here"). Charming individually; as a system it means a reader never learns the control.

**Fix.** Two cheap moves that don't break the rule. (a) Standardise the cue to one label and give it a weight the reader can find — it can stay small and stay gray, but it should sit under the lede rather than under the meta grid, where the eye already is. (b) Say *what* is under it: `· 4 more paragraphs ↓` or `· what i touched — 3 levels ↓`. A disclosure that declares its payload gets opened; one that says "more" does not.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] A convinced reviewer has nothing to do

**Why it matters.** Suppose the site works and a design lead wants to act. Their options: one `mailto:` in 9px mono at the bottom of the page. No LinkedIn, no resume, no location, no "open to work" state, no dates. For a site whose entire purpose is getting hired, the conversion path is the least designed thing on it. The email is also below the drawer, which means a reviewer who bounces at the folder never sees it.

**Fix.** The foot is the right place, but the address deserves the weight of a real destination rather than a footnote — and it should carry whatever else is true (LinkedIn, a CV link, city). If a resume PDF is out of scope, say so; but "email only, 9px, page bottom" is a decision by omission rather than by design.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

## Minor Observations

- **Pink at rest, in dark mode.** On `/motion-dime` the burgundy accent resolves to a value close enough to `--rose` that the `01` / `02` numerals read as the interaction accent sitting at rest — the Last-2% Rule's visual promise, if not its letter. Worth an eye check against the olive and navy records, which don't have this collision.
- **Redundant labelling inside the cover.** `RECORD 01 · VPRO` (eyebrow) → `VPro redesign` (title) → `4 CHAPTERS` (right eyebrow) → `4 chapters, three years` (well head, ~40px above) → `00 · THE OLD SYSTEM` (eyebrow) → `The old system` (h4). Six labels, three facts. Density 8 is meant to be earned with substance; some of this is earned with repetition.
- **Hierarchy inversion in the chapter band.** The meta grid spans the full ~1040px measure while the body prose — the most important content — is set at 14.5px on a 62ch column hugging the left edge, with the right 45% empty. The least important element is the widest.
- **Shelf pages don't fill the viewport.** `/writing` and `/reading` end their footer mid-screen on a tall display with dead ground below it. A `min-height` on the shell would push the foot down.
- **`03 Tasks`' lede renders as `[level-1 line not yet written — to be drawn from the Tasks material, not forced]`** on the public route. Correct per your own TODO discipline, and still a visible TODO in the flagship.
